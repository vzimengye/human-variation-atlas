 d.  Climate Crisis: Mesoamerican Megadrought 
 i.  Around 900–1100 CE, a major megadrought occurred in Mesoamerica. 
 ii.  Main Q: Did this drought cause population replacement? 
 1.  Researchers analyzed: Genome wide data for 12 ancient individuals from across Mexico + 
 Samples from multiple regions of Mexico 
 2.  Foundings: 
 a.  Long-term genetic stability: The genetic structure remained stable for at least 1,400 years. 
 b.  Population continuity: in Sierra Gorda despite the 900-1100 CE climate change (droughts) 
 3.  Reasons: Climatic conditions in SG and cinnabar mining might have favored the permanence of 
 populations despite the droughts. (Two factors: weather - instable; people - mining) 
 e.  Summmary 
 i.  Pre-Hispanic Mexican populations follow a northwest → southeast genetic cline. 
 ii.  Genetic structure remained stable for ~1,400 years. 
 iii.  No population replacement after the megadrought in Sierra Gorda. 
 iv.  Ancient DNA research in Mexico is feasible and informative. 
 4.  Part II: The genetic legacy of Colonization in Mexico 
 a.  Spanish Conquest and Colonization 
 i.  Key historical event: 1521 — Spanish conquest of Tenochtitlán 
 ii.  Consequences: Major demographic transformation+Mixing of populations from three continents 
 iii.  Populations involved: Indigenous Americans; Europeans (mainly Spanish); Africans (via slavery) 
 b.  Colonialism in Mexico produced a mosaic of different continental ancestries 
 i.  Population genetics methods used: PCA (Principal Component Analysis); Global ancestry 
 datasets (e.g., PAGE study, 1000 Genomes) 
 ii.  Goal: Use ancient and modern genomes to understand the impact of colonization through time. 
 c.  Sex-biased admixture 
 i.  Archaeological samples from  Colonial Mexico City 
 1.  Human remains from colonial Mexico City/ Archaeological sites such as La Conchita 
 2.  Sample collection; Ancient DNA extraction,  library construction, and NGS 
 3.  Genetic results:Individuals show 3-way admixture: Indigenous, European, African 
 4.  Maternal ancestry was largely Indigenous. 
 ii.  Colonial mixing was not equal between sexes <=  Genetic consequence of Colonialism 1: 
 Sex-biased admixture of Indigenous women with non-Indigenous men. 
 d.  Forced displacement of Africans into ”New Spain” 
 i.  Previous evidence of  African-ancestry individuals in the Colonial Hospital  : African enslaved 
 people came from diverse regions of Africa 
 ii.  Genetic consequence of Colonialism 2:  Forced displacement of individuals from Africa into 
 New Spain   => Trade of enslaved people from Africa to New Spain; More Africans than 
 Europeans in New Spain. 
 e.  African genetic ancestry in present-day Mexican populations 


--- PAGE 9 ---
 i.  Work with Afromexican communities from Veracruz and Costa Chica 
 ii.  African mitochondrial lineages are more frequent in Veracruz than in the Costa Chica 
 iii.  African Y chr lineages lineages are more frequent in Costa Chica than in Veracruz 
 iv.  Genetic consequence of Colonialism 3:  Admixture with forcibly displaced African individuals 
 from different continental regions. 
 f.  Summary 
 1.  Spanish colonization drastically reshaped Mexico’s demographic landscape. 
 2.  Admixture was sex-biased (Indigenous women + non-Indigenous men). 
 3.  The Transatlantic Slave Trade strongly influenced Mexican genetic diversity. 
 4.  Afro-Mexican ancestry traces back to multiple African regions.
